EU AI Act AI literacy training: what small businesses need to know

Article 4 of the EU AI Act asks businesses that use AI to support their staff's AI literacy. Here is what the European Commission's own Q&A says, in plain English.

Everything below comes from the European Commission's AI Literacy – Questions & Answers page (read October 25, 2026). It is a summary, not legal advice. Read the Q&A yourself and get advice for your situation.

What the rule says

  • Article 4 requires providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff and other people dealing with AI systems on their behalf. The Commission's examples include ordinary businesses whose staff use tools like ChatGPT (see below).
  • Article 4 has applied since 2 February 2025. Supervision and enforcement rules apply from 2 August 2026, through national market surveillance authorities.
  • The Digital Omnibus amendments entered into force in mid-July 2026. AI literacy remains an obligation, but no specific or "sufficient" level is mandated.
  • It can apply to businesses outside the EU when the AI system is used in the Union or its use affects people in the EU.

Does using ChatGPT count?

The Commission gives this exact example: a company whose employees use ChatGPT to write advertising text or translate. Its answer is "Yes, they should be informed about the specific risks, for example hallucination."

What training should cover

The Q&A suggests covering: what AI is and how it works, what AI is used in your organisation, its opportunities and dangers, whether you are a provider or a deployer, and the risks involved. It says training should be tailored to your staff's existing knowledge and the context the AI is used in.

Certificates and records

No certificate is needed. The Commission says organisations can keep an internal record of trainings and other guiding initiatives. A dated sign-off sheet for each session is a simple way to keep that record.

Fines are not covered here: the Q&A says penalties are set by national law, so check the rules in each country where you operate.

Prompt 1

Map what AI your business uses

Help me prepare for AI literacy training under Article 4 of the EU AI Act. Ask me, one question at a time, which AI tools my staff use, for which tasks, and whether any of those uses affect customers or people in the EU. Then list the tools and uses in a table, and tell me which questions I should check against the European Commission's AI Literacy Q&A. Do not tell me what the law requires beyond what I give you.

Paste the relevant parts of the Commission Q&A into the chat if you want the AI to work from the source.

Prompt 2

Plan a one-hour session

Plan a 60-minute AI literacy session for [number] staff at a [type of business] who use [tools] for [tasks]. Cover: what AI is and how it works, what AI we use and why, opportunities and dangers (including made-up answers, often called hallucinations), our data rules, and when a human must check the output. Include timings, one group exercise using examples from our work, and 5 quiz questions.

Tailor the examples to your team. The Commission says training should fit staff knowledge and context.

Prompt 3

Create a training record sheet

Create a simple training record table I can print or keep in a spreadsheet: employee name, role, date trained, session title, trainer, tools covered, quiz score, signature, next refresher date. Add a short header paragraph explaining it is an internal record of AI literacy training.

Keep it with your AI policy and update it when people join.

Example (illustrative)

Business: A 12-person travel agency in Ireland. Staff use ChatGPT to draft itineraries and translate emails.

Role: Deployer: they use AI tools, they do not build them.

Training: One hour on how the tools work, made-up facts, the data traffic light and checking prices and dates before sending.

Record: A dated sign-off sheet per session, kept with the AI policy.

FAQ

Do I need an AI literacy certificate?

No. According to the European Commission's Q&A, no certificate is needed, and organisations can keep an internal record of trainings and other guiding initiatives.

When did the AI literacy rule start?

Article 4 entered into application on 2 February 2025. Supervision and enforcement rules apply from 2 August 2026.

Does it apply to a business outside the EU?

The Commission says it can apply to actors outside the EU where the AI system is used in the Union or its use affects people in the EU. Get advice for your situation.

What level of AI literacy is required?

After the Digital Omnibus amendments (in force mid-July 2026), AI literacy remains an obligation, but no specific or "sufficient" level is mandated.
