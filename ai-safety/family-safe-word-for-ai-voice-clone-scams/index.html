How to make a family safe word for AI voice-clone scams

Scammers can copy a loved one's voice from a short audio clip. A family safe word plus one rule, hang up and call back, is the simplest defence. Here is how to set it up in 20 minutes.

The FBI has warned that "criminals generate short audio clips containing a loved one's voice to impersonate a close relative in a crisis situation" (FBI IC3 PSA I-120324-PSA, Dec 3, 2024). Its advice: create a secret word or phrase with your family, and hang up and call the person directly. The FTC gives the same advice about fake emergency calls (FTC).

Imposter scams were the most reported kind of fraud in 2025: the FTC received more than 1 million imposter-scam reports, with more than $3.5 billion lost (FTC, March 2026). People aged 60 and over reported $4.885 billion in losses to the FBI in 2024 (FBI IC3 2024 report).

How to pick a good safe word

  • Choose it together, in person. Never send it by text, email or social media.
  • Make it something a stranger could not guess or find online: not a pet's name, street, birthday or team you post about.
  • Two unrelated words work well, for example "purple lighthouse" or "pancake tractor".
  • Make sure grandparents and kids can remember it. Write it on a wallet card if that helps, but do not label it.

How to use it on a call

  1. Pause. Urgent + money + "keep this secret" means stop.
  2. Ask "What's our family word?" Never say it first.
  3. Hang up and call back on a number you already have, even if the word was right.
  4. Tell someone else in the family before you send anything, even if the caller said not to.

No safe word? The FTC suggests asking a question only the real person would know, and calling someone else even if you were told to keep it secret. This page is general safety information, not legal or financial advice.

Prompt 1

Brainstorm safe words that are hard to guess

Suggest 20 family safe words or two-word phrases that are easy to remember but hard for a stranger to guess. Avoid anything that could be found online about a family, such as pet names, street names, birthdays, schools or sports teams. Mix silly and simple options that a child and a grandparent could both remember.

Pick one together offline. Do not tell the AI chat which one you chose.

Prompt 2

Write a family meeting script

Write a friendly 20-minute family meeting plan to set up a safe word against AI voice-clone scams. The family includes [describe, e.g. two parents, teenagers, a grandparent who lives alone]. Explain the threat calmly, without scaring anyone, then cover: picking the word, the hang-up-and-call-back rule, which phone numbers to save, and a quick role-play. Keep the language simple.

Do the role-play. Practising once makes it much easier to hang up for real.

Prompt 3

Practise a fake emergency call

Role-play a scam call so I can practise. You play a caller pretending to be my [grandson / daughter / boss] in an emergency who needs money right now and asks me to keep it secret. Stay in character and push back when I ask for our family word or say I will call back. After 6 exchanges, stop and tell me what I did well and what to improve.

Never share your real safe word in the chat. Use a pretend one.

Example (illustrative)

The call: "Grandma, it's me. I've been in an accident and I need $2,000 for bail. Please don't tell Mom."

Pause: Urgent, money, secret: all three red flags.

Ask: "What's our family word?" The caller says the line is bad and they need the money now.

Hang up and call back: Grandma calls her grandson's own number. He is at school and fine.

FAQ

Does a family safe word really work against AI voice cloning?

A cloned voice can sound right, but a scammer will not know a word your family chose in person. The FBI and FTC both recommend a family secret word or question, together with hanging up and calling back on a number you know.

What if the caller knows the safe word?

Hang up and call back anyway on a number you already have. Treat the word as one check, not the only one.

Should we change our safe word?

Change it if you think anyone outside the family has heard it, or after it has been used in a real situation.

Where do I report a voice-clone scam call?

Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and, for scams that happen online or through apps, at ic3.gov.
