How to write an AI policy for a small business

Your staff are probably already using ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini. Here is how to turn that into a short, clear policy in an afternoon, with copy-paste prompts to draft it.

A small-business AI policy does not need to be 40 pages. It needs to answer five questions your staff actually have: which AI tools they can use, what they may and may not paste into them, when a human has to check the output, when to tell customers, and who to ask when something goes wrong.

The biggest everyday risk is data. Customer details, staff records, health or payment information and anything confidential should never go into a public AI chat. A simple way to explain this is a data traffic light: green (public or harmless, fine in any approved tool), yellow (internal, company-approved tools only) and red (customer, staff, health, payment and confidential data: never in public AI tools).

The second risk is trusting output that is wrong. AI tools can state made-up facts with confidence. The European Commission's own guidance uses exactly this example: when employees use ChatGPT for ad text or translation, they should be informed about the specific risks, "for example hallucination" (European Commission, AI Literacy Q&A).

What to put in the policy

  • Purpose and scope: who it covers (employees, contractors) and which tools.
  • Approved tools: a short list, and how to ask for a new one.
  • Data rules: the green / yellow / red traffic light, with examples from your business.
  • Human review: anything that goes to a customer, or that involves money, legal, health or hiring decisions, is checked by a person.
  • Disclosure: when you tell customers AI was used.
  • Intellectual property and security: who owns output, no sharing logins, use company accounts.
  • Prohibited uses: for example, decisions about people made by AI alone.
  • Incident reporting: what to do if someone pasted red data or sent a wrong answer.
  • Training and review: everyone is trained and signs off; the policy is reviewed on a set date.

Then train people. A policy nobody has heard about does not change behaviour. One short session with real examples from your work, a quick quiz and a signed acknowledgment is enough for most small teams, and it gives you your own record of who was trained and when.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Rules on AI, privacy and employment differ by country, state and industry, so have a qualified lawyer review your policy before you adopt it, especially if you handle health, financial or children's data.

Copy-paste prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your own details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot or Grok. Leave out names, account numbers and anything private.

Prompt 1

List how your team really uses AI

I run a [type of business] with [number] staff. Help me write an AI use policy. First, interview me: ask up to 10 short questions, one at a time, about which AI tools my staff use, what tasks they use them for, what kinds of customer or company data they handle, and any mistakes or worries so far. Then summarise my answers as a table: task, tool, data involved, risk level (low / medium / high).

Answer honestly. The policy only works if it matches what people already do.

Prompt 2

Build your data traffic light

Using the table above, sort the kinds of information my business handles into three groups. GREEN: public or harmless, fine in any approved AI tool. YELLOW: internal, only in company-approved tools with a business account. RED: never in any public AI tool (customer personal data, staff records, health, payment or confidential information). Give 4 to 6 real examples from my business for each colour, written so a new employee would understand.

Print the result as a one-page sheet. It is the part staff will remember.

Prompt 3

Draft the policy in plain English

Draft a short AI acceptable use policy for my business, no longer than 2 pages, in plain English. Sections: purpose and scope, approved tools (leave [brackets] for me to fill in), data rules using my traffic light, human review, telling customers, intellectual property, security, prohibited uses, reporting mistakes, training and sign-off, and review date. Do not invent laws or claim the policy makes me compliant with anything. Mark anything I should check with a lawyer as [CHECK].

Read every [CHECK] line carefully. AI tools are not lawyers.

Prompt 4

Write a 10-question staff quiz

Write a 10-question multiple-choice quiz to check that staff understood this AI policy: [paste policy]. Include at least 4 questions where the employee must decide if a piece of information is green, yellow or red. Put the answer key at the end, separately.

Use it at the end of a short training session, then keep the signed results.

Example (illustrative)

Business: A 6-person bookkeeping firm.

Green: Rewriting a newsletter paragraph about tax deadlines.

Yellow: Summarising internal meeting notes in the firm's business Copilot account.

Red: Pasting a client's bank statement into a free chatbot. Never.

Human review: Any AI-drafted email to a client is read by the accountant before it is sent.

FAQ

Do small businesses need an AI policy?

If staff use AI tools for work, a short written policy helps everyone know the rules on data, accuracy and disclosure. Whether a specific law requires one depends on where you operate and your industry, so check your situation with a lawyer.

How long should a small-business AI policy be?

Short enough that people read it. One or two pages of plain rules, plus a one-page data traffic light, works for most small teams.

Should we ban ChatGPT at work?

A total ban is hard to enforce and staff may use personal accounts anyway. Many small businesses instead approve specific tools, set data rules and require human review of anything customer-facing.

Does the EU AI Act require AI training?

Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff. See our guide on the AI literacy requirement for what the European Commission says.
