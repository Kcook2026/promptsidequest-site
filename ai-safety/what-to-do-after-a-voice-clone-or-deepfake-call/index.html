What to do after a voice-clone or deepfake scam call

If a call or video chat from a "relative" or "boss" asked for money, act fast. Here are the steps from the FTC and FBI, in order, whether or not you paid.

The FBI has warned that criminals use AI-generated voices of relatives in fake emergencies, and real-time video chats with fake company executives (FBI IC3 PSA, Dec 3, 2024). In 2024 the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center received 859,532 complaints with $16.6 billion in reported losses (IC3 2024 report).

If you did not pay

  • Hang up, then call the person back on a number you already have.
  • Tell someone else in the family or at work, even if the caller said to keep it secret.
  • Report the call at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

If money or information went out

The FTC's advice is to contact the company you paid through right away, report the fraud, and ask them to reverse the payment (FTC, What To Do if You Were Scammed):

  • Credit or debit card, bank transfer, payment app, wire: call your bank, card issuer or app now and ask them to reverse the transaction. The FTC notes federal law protects you from unauthorized use of your credit card and debit card.
  • Gift card: call the number on the back of the card, and keep the card and the receipt.
  • Cash by mail: call the U.S. Postal Inspection Service at 1-877-876-2455. Sent with FedEx or UPS? Contact them as soon as possible.
  • Crypto: contact the exchange or crypto ATM operator.
  • Gave your Social Security number? Go to IdentityTheft.gov.
  • Gave a password or remote access? Update security software, run a scan, change passwords and turn on two-step verification.

Then lock things down and report

  • Freeze your credit with all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). The FTC says a freeze is free, does not affect your credit score and lasts until you lift it (FTC).
  • Report to ReportFraud.ftc.gov, to ic3.gov for online scams, and to your state attorney general.
  • Beware the second scam: government agencies and legitimate organizations will never ask for money to help you get a refund (FTC, Refund and Recovery Scams).

General safety information, not legal or financial advice. Your protections and time limits depend on your bank, card issuer and account agreement.

Prompt 1

Write down what happened while it's fresh

I think I just got a scam call using an AI-cloned voice or a deepfake video. Help me write a clear incident note. Ask me, one at a time: date and time, the number or account that contacted me, who they pretended to be, what they said, what they asked for, whether I paid or shared anything, and how (card, bank transfer, app, gift card, crypto, cash). Then turn my answers into a short timeline I can read to my bank and paste into a fraud report.

Leave out full card or account numbers in the chat. Add them by hand later.

Prompt 2

Plan the calls to make, in order

Based on this incident note: [paste note], list the calls and reports I should make, in order of urgency, using this guidance from the FTC: contact the bank, card issuer or payment app first and ask to reverse the payment; gift cards: call the number on the back and keep the card and receipt; cash by mail: U.S. Postal Inspection Service; SSN shared: IdentityTheft.gov; then ReportFraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov. Give me a short script for the bank call.

Call your bank first. Speed matters most there.

Prompt 3

Draft a dispute message to your bank

Draft a short, factual message to my bank asking them to investigate and reverse a fraudulent payment. Details: [date, amount, payment method, recipient if known, how the scam worked]. Ask them to confirm in writing what happens next and any reference number. Do not claim legal rights I have not told you about.

Check your account agreement for time limits, and keep copies of everything.

Example (illustrative)

The call: A video call from the "CEO" asks the office manager to wire $9,500 to a new supplier today.

Red flag: Urgent payment, new account details, pressure to skip normal checks.

Step 1: The manager calls the CEO on the number in the company directory. The CEO knew nothing.

Step 2: The wire had already gone, so the manager calls the bank immediately to ask for a recall, then reports it at ic3.gov.

FAQ

Can I get my money back after a voice-clone scam?

Sometimes. The FTC says to contact your bank, card issuer or payment app right away and ask them to reverse the payment. Results depend on the payment method, how fast you act and your account agreement.

Where do I report a deepfake or voice-clone scam?

Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and, for scams online or by app, at ic3.gov. You can also report to your state attorney general.

Someone offered to recover my money for a fee. Is that real?

Almost certainly a scam. The FTC says government agencies and legitimate organizations will never ask for money to help you get a refund.

Should I freeze my credit?

If you shared personal information, a credit freeze is a strong step. The FTC says it is free, does not affect your score and lasts until you lift it; contact all three bureaus.
