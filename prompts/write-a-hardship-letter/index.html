ChatGPT prompt to write a hardship letter

Five copy-paste prompts to explain what changed, ask for a specific kind of help, write a calm hardship letter and follow up until you get an answer.

A hardship letter is a short, honest explanation of why you cannot pay a bill the usual way right now, and what you are asking for instead. People send them to hospitals about medical bills, to card issuers and lenders, and to landlords about rent, usually after a job loss, illness, cut in hours or family emergency.

Many hospitals, lenders and landlords have their own hardship or financial-assistance process, and some will only consider a request that goes through it. A clear letter still helps: it shows what happened, what you can realistically pay and exactly what you are asking for.

An AI chat cannot see your accounts, promise an outcome or tell you what rules apply where you live. It is good at turning a stressful situation into a short timeline, matching your request to what you can afford, and drafting a polite letter that does not over-share. Remove account numbers, your full address, date of birth and medical details you would not want shared before you paste anything. This page is not legal or financial advice.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Turn what happened into a short timeline

Help me write a hardship letter. Here is what changed, in my own words: [describe, e.g. I lost my job in [month]; I was in hospital for [length of time]; my hours were cut]. Turn this into a short, factual timeline with dates or months, what happened, and how it affected my income or expenses. Keep it to 3 to 6 points. Leave out anything that is not needed to explain why I cannot pay as usual; ask me if something important is missing instead of guessing.

Stick to facts you can back up. You do not need to share private medical details; "a medical issue that kept me out of work" is usually enough.

Prompt 2

Work out what you can really pay

Here is my monthly income and my essential costs right now: [list income and costs, e.g. rent, food, utilities, transport, minimum payments]. Here is the bill or account I am writing about: [type of bill, balance, normal payment]. Put this in a simple table, show what is left each month, and suggest 2 or 3 realistic requests I could make, such as a smaller monthly payment, a short pause, a reduced balance or a later due date. Do not suggest anything I cannot afford, and do not invent programs, rates or rules.

Only offer an amount you can keep paying every month.

Prompt 3

Find out what to ask for and who to send it to

I need to send a hardship request to [hospital or clinic / card issuer or lender / landlord or property manager]. Write a short list of questions I should ask them first, for example: do you have a hardship or financial-assistance process, is there a form or application, what documents do you need, who should the letter go to, and how will I hear back. Then write a short phone or email script to ask them.

Look on their website or your statement for hardship, financial assistance or payment arrangements. If they have an official form, use it and attach your letter.

Prompt 4

Write the hardship letter

Write a hardship letter to [who it is going to] about [the bill, card or rent]. Use this timeline: [paste from Prompt 1] and this request: [paste the request you chose from Prompt 2]. Include placeholders for my name, the account or reference number and the date. Explain what happened in 3 or 4 sentences, say clearly what I am asking for and for how long, mention what I have already done to cope, list any documents I am attaching, and ask them to reply in writing. Keep it under 300 words, honest and calm, with no legal threats and no claims about laws or deadlines.

Read it out loud and fix anything that is not quite true. Keep a copy of everything you send.

Prompt 5

Follow up and keep records

Make a simple tracking table for my hardship request with these columns: who I contacted, date, how (phone, email, letter, online form), name of the person I spoke to, reference number, what they said, documents sent, and next step. Fill in this row: [details]. Then write a short, polite follow-up message I can send if I do not hear back, and another if they say no, asking whether there is any other option, such as a payment plan or a review later.

Get any agreement in writing and read the terms before you pay under it.

Worked example

Situation: a renter whose hours were cut for two months is behind on a hospital bill and worried about next month's rent.

What you paste: a few sentences about the reduced hours into Prompt 1 and their monthly numbers into Prompt 2. The AI builds a short timeline and a budget table, then suggests asking the hospital for a smaller monthly payment and asking the landlord to split one month's rent across two payments.

Next: Prompt 3 gives them questions to ask the hospital's billing office, which points them to its financial-assistance application. Prompt 4 drafts one short letter for the hospital and one for the landlord, and Prompt 5 sets up a table with follow-up dates. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of any outcome.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT write a hardship letter for me?

It can draft one from the facts you give it, but you should check every line, make sure it is true and add your own details. It cannot contact the hospital, lender or landlord for you or guarantee they will agree. You send the letter yourself, ideally through their own hardship or financial-assistance process.

What should a hardship letter include?

What changed and when, how it affects your ability to pay, a specific and realistic request, what you have done to cope, any attached documents, and how to reach you. Keep it brief, factual and polite.

What if they say no?

Ask whether there is another option, such as a payment plan, a later review or a different form to fill in, and ask for the decision in writing. For advice about your situation, look for a nonprofit credit counselor, a legal aid service or your local consumer protection agency.
