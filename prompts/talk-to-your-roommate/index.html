ChatGPT prompt to talk to your roommate about chores

Five copy-paste prompts to plan what to say about dishes, cleaning, noise or bills, practice the talk and agree on something that sticks.

Most roommate problems start small: a sink full of dishes, a bathroom nobody cleans, music at 1 a.m., a bill that gets paid late. They stay small right up until someone finally snaps. The fix is usually one honest, calm conversation, and the hard part is knowing how to start it without sounding like a parent or a lawyer.

AI is useful here in three ways. It helps you separate the facts from the frustration, it gives you an opening line that does not put the other person on defense, and it lets you rehearse with someone who can play your roommate before you talk to the real one.

Keep it fair. Describe what happens and how it affects you, not what kind of person your roommate is. Leave out full names and private details about them. The AI only needs the situation.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Sort out what is really bugging you

I want to talk to my roommate about a problem. Here is what has been happening, in my own messy words: [describe it, e.g. dishes left in the sink for days, I end up doing them]. Help me separate the specific facts from my feelings and assumptions. Then tell me the one main thing I should ask for, and what I can let go of for now.

Start here when you are still annoyed. It turns a long list of complaints into one clear ask.

Prompt 2

Opening line and talking points

Help me plan what to say to my roommate about [issue]. Give me a friendly opening line that does not sound like an attack, 3 short points that explain my side calmly using "I" statements, and one specific request. Keep it warm and honest, not corporate. We have lived together for [time] and generally get along [well / okay / not great].

Pick the version that sounds most like you and say it in your own words.

Prompt 3

If they get defensive

I am about to talk to my roommate about [issue]. List the 5 most likely defensive replies, such as "you leave stuff out too" or "I have been really busy". For each one, give me a calm response that acknowledges their point without dropping my request. No sarcasm and no scorekeeping.

Read these before the talk so a defensive reply does not throw you off.

Prompt 4

Practice round

Play my roommate in a conversation about [issue]. Be realistic: a little defensive at first, then open to a fair deal if I stay calm. Reply one message at a time and wait for me. After 6 exchanges, step out of the role and tell me what I did well and one thing to say differently.

Do this once or twice. It takes the nerves out of the first sentence.

Prompt 5

Turn it into a simple agreement

My roommate and I agreed to the following about [chores / quiet hours / bills / guests]: [paste what you agreed]. Turn it into a short, friendly shared note we can both keep, with who does what, how often, and when we will check in on how it is going. Keep it under 120 words and make it sound like two friends, not a lease.

A written note stops the same argument from coming back next month.

Worked example

Situation: two roommates share a kitchen. One cooks most nights and leaves pans soaking for days, and the other keeps quietly washing them and getting more annoyed.

What you paste: that description into Prompt 1. The AI points out that the real ask is "pans get washed the same night", not a debate about who is messier, and suggests letting the occasional mug go.

Next: Prompt 2 gives an opener like "Hey, can we figure out a kitchen system? I have been getting a bit stressed about the pans." Prompt 3 prepares a reply for "you never take the trash out", which is to agree, offer to own the trash, and ask about the pans. Prompt 5 turns the deal into a short note on the fridge with a two-week check-in.

FAQ

How do I bring up chores with my roommate without starting a fight?

Pick a calm moment, not right after you find the mess. Start with something you both want, like a kitchen that works, describe the specific problem in one or two sentences, and end with a clear, small request. Then listen.

Should I text my roommate or talk in person?

In person is usually better for anything that has been building up, because tone is easier to read. A short text is fine to ask for a time to talk, and for writing down what you agreed afterward.

What if the conversation does not change anything?

Give it a week or two, then check in once using the note you agreed on. If the problem continues, ask the AI to help you plan a firmer second conversation or a fair cleaning rota. If you rent, your lease may also cover shared spaces and bills.
