AI prompt for a hard conversation with your boss

Plan what to say, rehearse it with an AI playing your boss, and walk in with a clear ask.

Asking for a raise, pushing back on workload, owning a mistake or giving your manager feedback all have the same problem: you know what you feel, but the words come out wrong in the moment. AI is a surprisingly good rehearsal partner. It doesn't judge, it doesn't get tired, and it can play a skeptical boss as many times as you need.

The prompts below take you from a messy feeling to a clear goal, an opening line, a few calm talking points and a practice run. Keep company secrets and other people's private details out of the chat. Describe the situation in general terms instead.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Get clear on the goal

I need to have a hard conversation with my boss about [topic, e.g. my workload / a raise / a mistake I made]. Here is the background: [2-4 sentences]. Ask me up to 5 questions, one at a time, to figure out what outcome I really want, what my boss probably cares about, and what I am willing to compromise on. Then summarize my goal in one sentence.

Answer honestly. The clearer the goal, the better every later prompt works.

Prompt 2

Conversation planner

Help me plan a conversation with my boss. My goal: [one sentence]. What my boss cares about: [e.g. deadlines, team morale, budget]. Give me: a calm opening line, 3 talking points with one fact or example each, a specific ask, and 2 options I could offer if they say no. Keep my tone [direct but friendly].

Bring real examples: dates, projects, numbers you can verify.

Prompt 3

Role-play rehearsal

Play my boss in a practice conversation. My boss is [describe style, e.g. busy, skeptical, data-driven]. The topic is [topic] and my goal is [goal]. Push back realistically, one reply at a time, and wait for my answer. After 6 exchanges, step out of character and tell me what I did well and 3 things to improve.

Run it twice: once with an easygoing boss and once with a tough one.

Prompt 4

Tough-reaction prep

List the 5 hardest things my boss might say when I bring up [topic], such as "now is not a good time" or "everyone is busy". For each, give me a calm, short reply that keeps the conversation moving toward [goal] without sounding defensive.

Memorize one or two replies rather than reading them word for word.

Prompt 5

Follow-up email

Write a short follow-up email to my boss after our conversation about [topic] on [date]. Thank them, summarize what we agreed: [agreements], list next steps with owners and dates, and keep it friendly and under 150 words.

A written recap protects both of you and makes the agreement real.

Worked example

Situation: you have taken on a second role's work for four months and want either help or a pay review.

Prompt 1 helps you see that you mainly want the workload fixed, and that a raise is your fallback. Prompt 2 gives you an opener: "I want to talk about how I can keep doing great work on X, because right now I'm covering two roles." Prompt 3 has a busy, budget-focused boss push back with "there's no headcount this quarter." You practice offering two options: move one project to someone else, or set up a compensation review next quarter. Prompt 5 writes the recap afterward.

FAQ

Can AI really help me talk to my boss?

It cannot have the conversation for you, but it is very good at turning a vague worry into a clear goal, an opening line and practice replies, which is where most people get stuck.

Should I tell my boss I used AI to prepare?

That is up to you. Preparing with AI is like rehearsing with a friend. What matters is that the words and facts you use are true and your own.

What should I not paste into the AI?

Leave out confidential company information, other people's names and private details, and anything covered by an NDA. Describe the situation in general terms instead.
