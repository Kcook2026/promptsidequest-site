ChatGPT prompt to negotiate a job offer

Five copy-paste prompts to work out a fair number, write a calm counteroffer, rehearse the call and ask for more than just salary, without risking the offer.

You finally have an offer, and now the hard part: do you take it as it is, or ask for more? A lot of people accept the first number because negotiating feels awkward or risky, even when a polite, well-reasoned counteroffer is a normal part of hiring.

An AI chat is a useful partner here. It can help you organize what you know about the role and your market, turn that into a clear ask, write an email that sounds confident rather than demanding, and play the recruiter so you can practice before the real call.

Two firm rules. First, the AI does not know real salaries for your job in your city, so check its suggestions against real sources such as job posts that list pay ranges, public salary surveys or people in your field. Second, everything you say has to be true: never let it invent a competing offer or experience you do not have.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Find your number

I received a job offer for [job title] at [company] in [city or remote]. Offer: [base salary, bonus, equity, benefits, start date]. My experience: [years, key skills, one or two results]. Pay ranges I found from real sources: [paste ranges from job posts or salary surveys, with the source]. Help me decide on three numbers: my target, a reasonable ask slightly above it, and my walk-away minimum. Explain your reasoning in plain language and tell me what other information would make this more reliable.

Bring real pay data. Without it, the AI is guessing and you should treat its numbers as a starting point only.

Prompt 2

The counteroffer email

Write a short, warm counteroffer email to [recruiter or hiring manager name] for the [job title] offer at [company]. Start by thanking them and saying I am excited about the role. Then ask for a base salary of [your ask] based on [one or two honest reasons, e.g. my experience with X, the pay ranges I found for similar roles]. Keep it under 150 words, confident but not demanding, and end by saying I would love to find a way to make this work. No ultimatums and no invented competing offers.

Short and friendly works better than a long list of reasons.

Prompt 3

Practice the call

Act as a recruiter at [company] who has offered me [offer] for [job title]. I will ask for [your ask]. Respond the way a real recruiter might, including pushback such as 'that is above our budget' or 'this is our best offer'. After each of my replies, give me one thing that worked and one phrase to improve. After five exchanges, summarize how I came across.

Use voice mode if you can. Saying the number out loud a few times makes the real call much easier.

Prompt 4

Negotiate beyond salary

The company says the base salary of [amount] is fixed for the [job title] role. Here is the full offer: [paste offer details]. Here is what matters most to me: [e.g. remote days, start date, extra vacation, a signing bonus, training budget, a pay review in 6 months]. Suggest the three things that are most realistic to ask for, and write one short, polite sentence I could use to ask for each.

When salary will not move, other parts of the offer sometimes can.

Prompt 5

Accept or decline gracefully

I have finished negotiating the [job title] offer at [company]. The final offer is [details], and I have decided to [accept / decline]. Write a short email that [accepts and asks them to send the final terms in writing / declines politely, thanks them for their time and keeps the door open]. Under 120 words, warm and professional.

Whatever you decide, get the final terms in writing before you resign from your current job.

Worked example

Situation: an example candidate gets an offer for a marketing coordinator role at a base salary of $52,000. Three local job posts for similar roles list ranges between $50,000 and $60,000.

What you paste: the offer, those three ranges with links, and two years of social media experience into Prompt 1. The AI suggests a target of $56,000, an ask of $57,000 and a walk-away of $53,000, and points out that one of the posts is for a more senior role.

Next: Prompt 2 writes a friendly 110-word email asking for $57,000. Prompt 3 rehearses the pushback, and when the recruiter says the budget is fixed, Prompt 4 helps the candidate ask for a pay review after six months instead. Prompt 5 writes the acceptance email asking for the final terms in writing.

FAQ

Is it okay to negotiate a job offer?

In many industries a polite counteroffer is a normal part of hiring, and recruiters often expect it. Keep it respectful, base your ask on real information and be ready to accept a no on some points. Norms vary by company, country and role, so use your judgment.

Can ChatGPT tell me what salary to ask for?

It can help you reason about a number, but it does not have reliable, current salary data for your exact job and city. Give it real pay ranges from job posts, salary surveys or people in your field, and check its suggestion against them.

Should I negotiate by email or on the phone?

Either can work. Email gives you time to choose your words and leaves a written record, while a call can feel more personal. Many people send a short email and are happy to talk it through on a call. Whatever you agree, ask for the final offer in writing.
