ChatGPT prompt to cancel a subscription and get a refund

Five copy-paste prompts to find what you are paying for, cancel without getting talked into staying, ask for a refund and follow up in writing.

Free trials that turned into monthly charges, an app you stopped using in spring, a streaming service you only needed for one show. Subscriptions are easy to start and easy to forget, and the cancel button is often harder to find than the sign-up button.

An AI chat is a handy helper for the boring parts. It can sort a bank statement into a list of recurring charges, write a short cancellation message that leaves no room for confusion, draft a polite refund request, and give you a script for the chat window or phone call where they try to keep you.

Two honest notes. Refunds are not guaranteed: they depend on the company's terms, how long ago you were charged and the rules where you live, so the AI cannot promise one. And never paste full card numbers, passwords or login codes into a chat. Merchant names, dates and amounts are all you need.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Find every subscription

Here are the last three months of my bank and card transactions with card numbers removed: [paste merchant names, dates and amounts]. Find every charge that looks recurring or like a subscription. Make a table with: merchant, amount, how often it appears, the next likely charge date, and a column where I can mark keep or cancel. Then add up the monthly and yearly total.

Three months catches quarterly charges too. Seeing the yearly total is usually the push you need.

Prompt 2

Short cancellation message

Write a short, clear message to [company] to cancel my [plan name] subscription on the account under [email you signed up with, add it yourself]. Say I want it cancelled effective immediately or at the end of the current billing period [choose one], that I do not want a discount or a pause, and ask for a written confirmation of the cancellation and the date of the last charge. Under 100 words, polite and firm.

Use the company's own cancel page or help form first, and save a screenshot or email of the confirmation.

Prompt 3

Polite refund request

I was charged [amount] by [company] on [date] for [plan]. Reason I am asking for a refund: [honest reason, e.g. I forgot to cancel the free trial and have not used the service since, I was charged after cancelling on (date), I was charged twice]. Write a short, friendly refund request that states the facts, says what I am asking for (a full or partial refund of [amount]) and thanks them. Under 150 words. No threats and no invented details.

Ask soon after the charge. Many companies are more flexible in the first days after a renewal.

Prompt 4

Retention chat script

I am about to cancel [service] by chat or phone and I expect them to offer discounts or a pause to keep me. Write me a short script: my opening line, and a polite one-sentence reply to each of these: 'we can give you 50% off', 'would you like to pause instead', 'can I ask why you are leaving', and 'you will lose your data'. Every reply should bring the conversation back to: please cancel and confirm in writing.

If a discount genuinely changes your mind, that is fine too. The point is that you decide, not the script.

Prompt 5

Follow up after a charge that should not have happened

I cancelled [service] on [date] and have confirmation [describe: email, screenshot, reference number], but I was charged [amount] again on [date]. Write a short follow-up message asking them to refund that charge and confirm the account is closed. Then list, in plain language, the next steps people usually take if a company does not respond, such as contacting my card issuer or bank about a disputed charge, and what records I should have ready.

Keep every confirmation. It is the strongest thing you have if you need to dispute a charge with your bank.

Worked example

Situation: an example person notices their bank balance is lower than expected and suspects old subscriptions.

What you paste: three months of transactions with card numbers removed into Prompt 1. The table shows seven recurring charges, including a photo-editing app and a fitness app the person forgot about, adding up to about $38 a month.

Next: they mark two to cancel. Prompt 2 writes the cancellation messages, Prompt 4 handles the fitness app's retention offer, and Prompt 3 asks the photo app for a refund of a renewal charged four days earlier. Whether a refund is given depends on the company; this is an illustrative example, not a promise.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT cancel my subscriptions for me?

No. A regular AI chat cannot log in to your accounts or click cancel. It helps you find subscriptions, write the messages and plan what to say. You still cancel through the company's website, app, app store or support channel.

Will I actually get a refund?

Sometimes. It depends on the company's refund policy, how long ago you were charged and local consumer rules. A short, honest, specific request sent soon after the charge gives you the best chance. If you were charged after cancelling, your confirmation is the key evidence.

How do I cancel a subscription bought through an app store?

Subscriptions bought inside an iPhone or Android app are usually managed in the App Store or Google Play subscription settings, not in the app itself. Ask the AI to walk you through the steps for your device, then check them against the official help page.
