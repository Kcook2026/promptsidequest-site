ChatGPT prompt to plan a trip

Five copy-paste prompts that turn your dates, budget and interests into a realistic day-by-day itinerary, a spending plan and a packing list, plus a backup plan for when things go sideways.

Planning a trip usually means a dozen open tabs, a half-finished list and a nagging feeling that you forgot something. The hard part is not finding ideas. It is fitting them into real days, with travel time, rest and a budget that does not run out halfway through.

An AI chat is good at that sorting job. Tell it where you are going, for how long, who is coming and what you actually enjoy, and it can group sights by neighborhood, pace the days so you are not rushing, split a budget into daily amounts and write a packing list for the weather and activities you described.

One important caution: AI chats can be out of date or simply wrong about opening hours, prices, transit routes and even whether a place still exists. Use the plan as a draft, then check each booking, ticket and timetable on the official website before you rely on it. For passports, visas, entry rules and travel advisories, always check the official government sources for your trip.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

The day-by-day itinerary

Plan a [number]-day trip to [city or region] for [who is going, e.g. two adults / family with kids aged 6 and 9]. We arrive [day and time] and leave [day and time]. We like [food, museums, hiking, nightlife, beaches, history] and want to avoid [long lines, early mornings, lots of driving]. Our pace is [relaxed / balanced / packed]. Group places that are near each other on the same day, include travel time between stops, leave one free block each day, and put it in a table with morning, afternoon and evening. Mark anything that usually needs booking ahead.

The core prompt. The more honest you are about pace and dislikes, the better the plan.

Prompt 2

The trip budget

My total budget for this trip is [amount and currency], not counting [flights, if already booked]. Break it into lodging, food, local transport, activities and a buffer for surprises. Then give me a daily spending amount and three ways to save money that fit the trip we planned, without cutting the things I said I care most about. Tell me which numbers are rough guesses I should check.

Asking it to flag guesses keeps estimated prices from looking like facts.

Prompt 3

The packing list

Make a packing list for [number] days in [place] in [month], for [who is going]. We will be doing [activities] and staying in [hotel / rental / camping]. We are bringing [carry-on only / checked bags]. Group the list by category, mark the things people most often forget, and keep it short enough to fit the luggage I described.

Re-run it a few days before you leave with the actual forecast.

Prompt 4

The rainy-day and backup plan

Here is my itinerary: [paste it]. For each day, suggest one indoor backup in case of bad weather and one quick swap if a place is closed or sold out. Also list what to do if a flight or train is delayed or cancelled: what to ask the airline or rail company, what to keep receipts for, and a short, polite message I can send to my hotel or rental host if I will arrive late.

Ten minutes now saves a stressful hour at the airport.

Prompt 5

The local etiquette and phrase sheet

I am visiting [country or city] for the first time. Give me a short cheat sheet: tipping customs, how to pay (cash or card), basic etiquette at restaurants and religious sites, how to get around from the airport, and 10 useful phrases in the local language with simple pronunciation. Keep it to one screen, and tell me which points I should double-check with an official or recent source.

Handy to save offline before you go.

Worked example

Situation: an example couple has four days in a city they have never visited, a fixed budget for everything except flights, and a habit of over-planning and coming home exhausted.

What you paste: their dates, arrival and departure times, interests and a "relaxed" pace into Prompt 1. The AI groups sights by neighborhood, keeps each day to two main stops plus a free block, and marks two places that usually need tickets booked ahead.

Next: Prompt 2 splits the budget into a daily amount with a buffer, and shows that eating lunch as the main meal fits it better than dinners out. Prompt 3 writes a carry-on packing list. Before booking, they check each ticket, opening time and transit route on the official sites and fix two items the AI had wrong.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT plan a trip itinerary for me?

Yes. Give it your destination, dates, arrival and departure times, budget, interests and preferred pace, and it can draft a day-by-day plan grouped by area, with travel time and free time built in. Treat it as a first draft and confirm opening hours, prices and bookings on official websites, because AI answers can be outdated or wrong.

Is AI travel information accurate?

Not always. AI chats can list places that have closed, give old prices or mix up transit details. Use the AI for structure and ideas, then check the important facts yourself. For passports, visas, entry requirements and safety advisories, rely only on official government sources.

What details should I give the AI for a better plan?

Your dates and times, who is traveling (including ages and mobility needs), where you are staying, your budget, what you enjoy, what you want to avoid, and how busy you like your days. Saying what you do not want, such as early starts or long drives, often improves the plan more than listing what you do want.
