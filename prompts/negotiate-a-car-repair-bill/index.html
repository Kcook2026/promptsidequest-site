ChatGPT prompt to question a car repair estimate

Five copy-paste prompts to decode a repair estimate, list questions before you authorize work, compare a second quote, ask for a clearer written estimate and log what you approved.

A car repair estimate can be hard to read: part names, labor hours, shop supplies and diagnostic fees stacked in a format built for the shop, not for you. Before you authorize work, it helps to understand what each line means, what is urgent versus what can wait, and whether a second shop is quoting the same diagnosis.

An AI chat is useful for the sorting work. Paste the estimate text with personal details removed, and it can translate line items, draft questions for the advisor, compare a second quote and help you request a revised written estimate. It cannot see the car, know whether a shop is overcharging, or tell you what the law requires where you live. Those answers come from the shop, a second opinion and, if needed, your insurer or a consumer protection agency.

Before pasting anything, remove your name, phone number, address, VIN, license plate and account or invoice numbers. Line descriptions, part names, labor hours and shop fee labels are enough. This page is not legal, financial or automotive advice, and it does not invent prices or claim any right to a discount.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Decode the estimate line by line

Explain this car repair estimate in plain English. Here is the text with my name, phone, address, VIN and invoice number removed: [paste estimate lines: part names, quantities, labor hours or labor descriptions, shop fees, diagnostic or supplies labels — do not invent dollar amounts I did not paste]. Make a table with: line as written, plain-English meaning, category (parts, labor, diagnostic, shop fee, tax or other), and a blank column for must-do / can-wait / ask. Flag anything unclear, duplicated or missing (for example labor without a matching part, or a fee with no explanation). Do not invent prices, parts or hours that are not in what I pasted. List questions I should ask the shop about any unclear lines.

Ask for a written itemized estimate before you authorize. A verbal total hides the lines you need to question.

Prompt 2

Questions before you authorize work

I have this decoded estimate: [paste the table from Prompt 1 or your own summary]. The shop diagnosed: [diagnosis in your words]. My priorities: [e.g. safety first, keep the car reliable for commuting, delay cosmetic work, use OEM vs aftermarket only if I say so]. List specific questions to ask the service advisor before I authorize anything. Cover: which items are safety-critical vs can wait, warranty on parts and labor, OEM vs aftermarket vs remanufactured parts, whether the diagnosis is confirmed or still a guess, what happens if they find more once they open things up, and how I get a revised written estimate if the scope changes. Do not invent shop policies, warranty lengths or prices.

Write the answers down (or ask for them by email) before you say yes to the work.

Prompt 3

Compare with a second quote

I have two car repair estimates for the same vehicle problem. Estimate A: [paste lines, personal info removed]. Estimate B: [paste lines, personal info removed]. Build a side-by-side checklist: same diagnosis?, same parts list?, same labor operations?, same exclusions or "may also need" notes?, same warranty language if either estimate includes it?. Flag mismatches I should ask about. Do not invent prices or pick a winner based on cost. If a line appears on only one estimate, mark it clearly. End with a short list of clarifying questions for each shop.

Same diagnosis and same parts list matter more than a lower total you cannot compare.

Prompt 4

Request a revised written estimate

Write a short, polite message or call script to [shop name] about estimate [date or RO number if I have one, else describe the visit]. I want: [e.g. a clearer written itemized estimate, an explanation of these lines: list them, a revised estimate after we remove can-wait items, confirmation of OEM vs aftermarket for these parts: list]. Keep it under 120 words, calm and specific. Ask them to reply in writing with the updated estimate before I authorize work. Do not invent discounts, deadlines, legal rights or prices.

A written revised estimate protects both you and the shop if the scope changes mid-job.

Prompt 5

Authorization and record log

Help me keep a clear record of this repair. Here is what I know: shop [name], vehicle [year/make/model only, no VIN], estimate date [date], items I am authorizing: [list], items I am declining or deferring: [list], warranty notes they gave me: [paste or write unknown], how I approved (signed estimate, email, phone — and with whom if known). Build a simple log I can save: date, what was authorized, what was declined, promised completion window if they stated one, parts type (OEM / aftermarket / unknown), warranty summary in their words, confirmation method and where I filed the written estimate. Leave blanks for unknowns. Do not invent terms I did not provide.

Save the written estimate and any approval email or signed copy with this log.

Worked example

Situation: someone gets a written estimate after a check-engine light visit. The sheet lists diagnostic time, several parts, labor hours and a shop supplies line, and they are not sure which items are required to fix the code versus optional while the car is already open.

What they do: Prompt 1 turns the pasted estimate text (name, phone and VIN removed) into a plain-English table with a must-do / can-wait / ask column. Prompt 2 produces questions about warranty, OEM versus aftermarket and what happens if more issues appear after teardown.

Next: they get a second written quote and run Prompt 3 to align diagnoses and parts lists, then send the Prompt 4 message asking for a revised written estimate that drops the deferred items. Prompt 5 becomes the authorization log they save with both estimates. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of any price, discount or outcome.

FAQ

Can AI tell me if the shop is overcharging?

No. An AI chat cannot see local labor rates, parts costs or the condition of your car. Use the estimate you received, ask clear questions and compare a second written quote for the same diagnosis. The shop and a second opinion are the sources for price and necessity — not the chat.

Is this legal advice?

No. These prompts help you organize questions and messages. They are not legal, financial or automotive advice. Warranty rules, lemon laws and consumer protections vary by place; check official sources or a qualified professional if a dispute is serious.

Is it safe to paste my repair estimate into an AI chat?

Paste only line descriptions, part names, labor hours and fee labels. Remove your name, phone number, address, VIN, license plate and invoice or account numbers first. Check your AI tool's privacy settings if you do not want chats saved or used for training.
