ChatGPT prompt to dispute a bill

Five copy-paste prompts that help you spot billing errors, write a firm dispute letter and stay calm on the phone.

A wrong bill is stressful because the company holds the paperwork and you hold the anxiety. AI evens that out a little. It can read an itemized statement line by line, point out charges worth questioning, and turn your frustration into a short, polite, specific letter that a billing department actually has to answer.

These prompts work for medical bills, phone and internet bills, utilities, subscriptions and credit card charges. One rule before you start: remove account numbers, your full date of birth and anything like a Social Security number before you paste a bill into any AI chat. The AI does not need them to help you.

AI is not a lawyer and can be wrong about rules in your state or country. Use it to get organized and find the right questions, then check anything important with the company, your insurer or a consumer protection agency.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Bill error finder

I am going to paste an itemized bill (with my personal details removed). Go through it line by line. Flag anything that looks like a duplicate charge, a charge for something I may not have received, an unusually high price, a fee that was never explained, or a math error. For each flag, explain in plain English why it might be wrong and what question I should ask the company. Bill:
[paste itemized bill]

Ask for the itemized version first. A summary bill hides the details you need.

Prompt 2

Dispute letter writer

Write a short, polite but firm dispute letter to [company name] about my bill dated [date] for [amount]. The charges I am disputing are: [list charges and why each is wrong]. I want them to [remove the charges / issue a refund / send an itemized bill / put the account on hold during the review]. Ask for a written response within [30] days. Keep it under 250 words, no threats, no legal jargon, and leave placeholders for my name and account number.

Short beats long. One clear request per letter gets faster answers.

Prompt 3

Phone call script

I am calling [company] to dispute a charge of [amount] for [reason]. Write me a phone script: a one-sentence opening, the key facts in 3 bullets, what to say if they say the charge is correct, how to politely ask for a supervisor, and a checklist of what to write down (name, date, reference number). Keep it calm and confident.

Keep the script open on your screen during the call so you don't lose your place.

Prompt 4

Rights and options check

I live in [state/country]. I am disputing a [medical / credit card / utility / phone] bill because [reason]. In plain English, what general consumer protections or dispute processes usually apply to this kind of bill? What deadlines should I watch for? List the official agencies or websites I should check to confirm. Tell me clearly which parts you are unsure about.

Treat this as a starting list to verify, not as legal advice.

Prompt 5

Follow-up escalation

I disputed a bill with [company] on [date] and [they have not replied / they denied it saying: paste their reply]. Write a polite follow-up that restates my dispute, references my first message and any reference number [number], and asks for a specific next step by [date]. Then list 3 escalation options I could use if this does not work.

Send follow-ups in writing so you have a record.

Worked example

Situation: a phone bill shows a $14.99 "premium services" charge three months in a row that you never signed up for.

What you paste: the three line items, with the account number removed, into Prompt 1. The AI flags the repeat charge as a possible unauthorized third-party subscription and suggests asking the carrier to remove it and block third-party billing.

Next: Prompt 2 produces a 150-word letter that asks for a $44.97 credit and a block on premium services. You send it through the carrier's chat, save the transcript, and use Prompt 5 if nobody replies within two weeks.

FAQ

Is it safe to paste my bill into ChatGPT?

Remove account numbers, your date of birth, ID numbers and anything you would not post publicly first. The AI only needs the line items, dates and amounts to help.

Can AI write a legally binding dispute letter?

It can write a clear letter, but it is not legal advice. For large amounts or deadlines that matter, check the rules with the company, your insurer or a consumer protection agency.

Which AI works best for disputing a bill?

Any of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok will work with these prompts. Give as much detail as you can and ask it to point out what it is unsure about.
