ChatGPT prompt to lower your electric bill

Five copy-paste prompts to understand your electric bill, find where the power goes, make a realistic savings plan and ask your utility the right questions.

Electric bills are hard to read. There is a usage charge, a delivery or distribution charge, fixed monthly fees, taxes and sometimes riders with names nobody explains. When the bill jumps, it is not obvious whether you used more power, the rate changed, or the bill was based on an estimate.

An AI chat cannot see your meter or know your utility's rates. It is good at turning a confusing bill into a plain list of charges, spotting what changed between months, guessing which appliances are worth checking first, turning that into a short plan you will actually follow, and writing clear questions for your utility.

Have your last two or three bills, the usage history from your utility's website or app if it has one, and a rough list of your big appliances and how you heat and cool your home. Remove your account number, meter number and address before pasting anything. Check every rate and program against your utility's own website before you act.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Decode the bill

Here is my electric bill with my account number, meter number and address removed: [paste bill]. List every charge in plain language, grouped into energy or usage charges, delivery or distribution charges, fixed monthly fees, taxes, and any riders or adjustments. Tell me how many kWh I used, the billing period, and whether the reading looks actual or estimated. If something is not clear from the bill, say so instead of guessing.

Ask for a table so you can compare the same lines across months.

Prompt 2

Find what changed

Here are my last few electric bills, numbers removed: [paste bill 1], [paste bill 2], [paste bill 3]. Compare them in a table: days in the billing period, kWh used, average kWh per day, total cost, and each charge type. Tell me whether my bill went up mainly because I used more power, because the price per kWh or the fees changed, because the billing period was longer, or because of an estimated reading. Use only the numbers I pasted.

Average use per day is fairer than the monthly total, because billing periods differ in length.

Prompt 3

Find the power hogs

Here is how my home uses electricity: home type [apartment/house, rough size], heating [type], cooling [type], water heater [electric/gas/unknown], and these appliances and habits: [list, e.g. old second fridge in garage, space heater in the office, dryer used daily, gaming PC on most evenings]. Rank which ones are most likely to be using the most electricity and why, and suggest a cheap or free way to check each one. Do not quote exact wattages or savings unless you clearly label them as rough estimates.

A plug-in power meter, the label on the appliance, or turning things off one at a time and watching your utility app are cheap ways to check.

Prompt 4

Build a savings plan

Based on my bill and the likely power hogs from the last answer, write a simple 30-day plan to lower my electric use. Split it into free changes (settings, habits, unplugging), low-cost fixes, and bigger decisions to research later. For each item, say what to do, how I will know if it worked, and anything to be careful about. Keep it to a one-page checklist and do not promise a specific amount of savings.

Change a few things at a time so you can see what actually moved the number on your next bill.

Prompt 5

Ask your utility the right questions

Write a short, polite message or call script for my electric utility, [Utility name]. I want to ask: whether my last reading was actual or estimated, which rate plans I am eligible for (for example time-of-use or budget billing), whether they offer free home energy checks or rebates, and whether there are assistance programs if my bill is hard to pay. Include my account details placeholder, keep it under 180 words, and end by asking them to confirm any change in writing. Do not invent programs or rates.

Programs and rate plans vary a lot by utility and location. Their website usually lists them, and the answer from the utility is what counts.

Worked example

Situation: a renter's winter bill nearly doubled and they assume the rate went up.

What you paste: three bills, numbers removed, into Prompts 1 and 2. The comparison shows the billing period was several days longer and daily use also rose, while the fees stayed about the same.

Next: Prompt 3 points to a space heater running all day in a home office as the first thing to check. Prompt 4 turns that into a short plan (heat the person, not the room, and use a timer), and Prompt 5 writes questions about budget billing and whether last month's reading was estimated. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of any result.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT tell me exactly how much I will save?

No. It does not know your utility's rates, your exact appliances or the weather ahead. Use it to understand the bill and decide what to check first, then let your next bills show what actually changed.

Is it safe to paste my electric bill into ChatGPT?

Remove your account number, meter number, address and anything you would not want shared before pasting. The charges, kWh and dates are enough for the AI to help you read the bill.

Should I switch to a time-of-use or budget billing plan?

It depends on when you use power and what your utility offers. Time-of-use plans can help if you can shift heavy use to cheaper hours, and budget billing spreads costs evenly but does not lower your total. Ask your utility which plans you qualify for and compare them with your usage history.
