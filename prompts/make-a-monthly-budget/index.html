ChatGPT prompt to make a monthly budget

Five copy-paste prompts that turn your take-home pay and bills into a simple monthly budget, find where the money leaks and plan for the costs that sneak up on you.

Most budgets fail for boring reasons: the categories are too detailed, the numbers are guesses, and one surprise bill wipes out the plan by the second week. A budget that works is usually short, built from what you actually spent last month, and has room for the things that only come up a few times a year.

An AI chat is good at the tedious part. Paste your income, fixed bills and a rough list of last month’s spending, and it can sort everything into a few categories, spot where the money went, and suggest a plan you can keep. It can also write the plan as a table you can copy into a spreadsheet or a notes app.

Two cautions. Do not paste account numbers, card numbers or full bank statements into an AI chat; totals and category names are enough. And the AI does not know your situation better than you do: treat its suggestions as a draft, and for debt, tax or investment decisions, check with a qualified professional or a free nonprofit credit counselor. This is help with planning, not financial advice.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

The simple monthly budget

Help me make a simple monthly budget. My take-home pay is [amount] per [month / paycheck, and how often I'm paid]. My fixed bills are: [rent or mortgage, utilities, phone, insurance, loan payments, subscriptions with amounts]. Last month I roughly spent [amounts] on [groceries, eating out, transport, shopping, other]. Group everything into no more than 6 categories, show what is left over or short, and suggest a monthly plan in a table I can copy. Keep it realistic, not strict.

The core prompt. Rough numbers are fine for the first pass; you can tighten them next month.

Prompt 2

Find the leaks

Here is my spending for the last [month / 30 days], by category or as a list of purchases: [paste totals or a list without account numbers]. Find the 3 biggest places my money goes that I might not notice, such as small repeat purchases, subscriptions or delivery fees. For each, suggest one easy change and estimate how much it could free up per month, using only the numbers I gave you.

Ask it to use your numbers only, so the savings estimate is not invented.

Prompt 3

Plan for irregular costs

List the costs I should plan for that do not come every month, based on my situation: [e.g. renter with a car and a pet, two kids, freelancer]. Include things like car repairs, annual subscriptions, gifts, medical costs and school fees. Ask me for a rough yearly amount for each, then turn the total into a monthly amount to set aside and add it to my budget as one "sinking fund" line.

This is the step most budgets skip, and why they break.

Prompt 4

Paycheck-by-paycheck plan

I'm paid [weekly / every two weeks / irregularly] and my income is about [amount, or a range if it changes]. My bills and due dates are: [list with dates]. Make a plan that tells me which bills to pay from which paycheck so I am never short before a due date. If my income changes month to month, build the plan around my lowest typical month and tell me what to do with any extra.

Good if the monthly view looks fine but you still run out before payday.

Prompt 5

Monthly check-in

Here is my budget plan: [paste it]. Here is what I actually spent this month: [totals by category]. Compare them in a short table, tell me in two sentences what went well, name the one category to adjust, and update next month’s plan. Do not lecture me; keep it practical.

Run it at the end of each month. Ten minutes is enough.

Worked example

Situation: an example renter takes home $3,200 a month, pays $1,350 rent plus about $600 in other fixed bills, and keeps running short in the last week before payday.

What you paste: those numbers and last month’s rough spending into Prompt 1. The AI groups everything into six categories, shows that groceries, eating out and delivery together are the biggest flexible cost, and drafts a plan with a set amount for each category and a small buffer.

Next: Prompt 3 adds a monthly set-aside for car repairs and yearly subscriptions, which explains the "surprise" shortfalls. Prompt 4 moves two bills to the second paycheck, so the last week stops being tight. At the end of the month, Prompt 5 compares the plan with what really happened.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT make a budget for me?

Yes. Give it your take-home pay, fixed bills and a rough idea of last month’s spending, and it can build a simple budget table, point out where money is going and suggest changes. Check the math yourself, since AI chats can make arithmetic mistakes, and keep the final plan in a spreadsheet or app you trust.

Is it safe to share my finances with an AI chat?

Share totals and category names, not account numbers, card numbers, passwords or full statements. Check your AI app’s privacy settings if you do not want your chats saved or used for training.

What budgeting method should I use?

Pick the simplest one you will keep up with. A common starting point is the 50/30/20 rule: about half of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and extra debt payments. You can ask the AI to compare it with zero-based budgeting or an envelope system using your own numbers and pick what fits.
