ChatGPT game master prompt for game night

Five copy-paste prompts that turn an AI chat into a game master for a paper-and-dice mystery adventure your group can play in one evening.

Tabletop role-playing games are a great game night, but someone usually has to spend hours preparing and then run the whole thing. An AI chat can take that job. Give it the number of players, how long you have and the mood you want, and it can write a short adventure and then run it, one scene at a time, reacting to what your group decides.

You do not need a rulebook or special dice. A normal six-sided die, some paper and pencils are enough. The prompts below keep the rules simple on purpose, so new players can join in within five minutes.

A few tips: put one person in charge of typing the group’s choices, keep the device where everyone can see it, and tell the AI to keep its replies short so the table does most of the talking. If a player does not want certain themes, say so in the first prompt.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Build the adventure

Create a mystery tabletop-style adventure for [4] players that takes about [90 minutes]. Just paper and dice, using one simple rule: roll a six-sided die, 4 or higher succeeds. Include the setup, one character for each player with a name, a skill and a secret, 3 key scenes, clues that point to the answer and a twist. Keep it [family-friendly / spooky / funny]. Do not reveal the solution yet.

This gives you the whole adventure in one go. Print or copy the character cards for each player.

Prompt 2

Act as the game master

Now act as the game master for this adventure. Describe each scene in 3 to 4 sentences, then ask the players what they do. When a player tries something risky, tell them to roll and wait for the result. Keep the story moving, give clues when players investigate, and never decide for the players.

Paste this right after Prompt 1, in the same chat, so the AI remembers the story.

Prompt 3

Stuck table rescue

The players are stuck in [scene] and nobody knows what to do next. Without giving away the solution, give the game master 3 subtle ways to nudge them: something in the environment, a character who shows up, and a clue they missed.

Use it when the table goes quiet for too long.

Prompt 4

Quick character maker

Make [2] extra characters for late arrivals to this adventure. Each needs a name, one sentence of background, one useful skill and one secret that connects them to the mystery. Make them easy to drop into the current scene.

Handy when someone shows up halfway through.

Prompt 5

The reveal and wrap-up

The players have made their final accusation: [what they decided]. As game master, reveal whether they were right, explain the full mystery including the twist, give each character a short ending based on what they did, and suggest one hook for a sequel next game night.

Ends the night on a high, and the sequel hook gives you next week’s plan.

Worked example

Situation: four friends with no tabletop experience want something different from board games on Saturday night, with about 90 minutes to play.

What you paste: Prompt 1 with 4 players, 90 minutes and a spooky but not scary tone. The AI writes a mystery set in a lighthouse during a storm, with a missing keeper, four characters who each hide a small secret, and a twist that the keeper staged the disappearance.

Next: Prompt 2 starts the game. When a player tries to pick the lock on the keeper’s desk, the AI asks for a roll. They roll a 2, so the lock jams and they hear footsteps instead. Midway the group gets stuck, Prompt 3 suggests a soaked logbook washing up, and Prompt 5 handles the reveal.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT really be a game master?

Yes, for short and simple games it works well. It describes scenes, plays the other characters and reacts to the players. Keep everything in one chat so it remembers the story, and remind it of key facts if it slips.

What do I need to play?

A phone, tablet or laptop with an AI chat, one six-sided die, paper and pencils. No rulebook is needed because the prompt uses a single simple dice rule.

Is this okay for kids or families?

Yes. Ask for a family-friendly tone and an age range in the first prompt, and an adult can read the AI’s replies out loud so they can skip or change anything.
