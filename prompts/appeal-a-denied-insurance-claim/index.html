ChatGPT prompt to appeal a denied insurance claim

Five copy-paste prompts to understand a claim denial, gather evidence, write a clear appeal letter and follow up.

A claim denial letter usually arrives with a short reason, a code or two and a paragraph about your right to appeal. It is easy to read it as final, but denials often come from missing paperwork, a coding mistake, a prior authorization problem or a medical-necessity question, and many of those can be challenged.

An AI chat cannot see your policy or records and cannot predict the outcome. It is good at translating the denial into plain language, turning your plan's rules into a checklist and drafting a calm letter that answers the stated reason point by point.

Gather the denial letter, the Explanation of Benefits (EOB) and your plan documents. Deadlines and steps differ by plan and location, so check your plan documents and the denial letter for the exact deadline. Remove your name, member ID, claim numbers and health details the AI does not need before pasting. This is help with writing, not legal or medical advice.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Decode the denial letter

Here is the text of an insurance claim denial letter with my personal details removed: [paste denial letter]. Explain in plain language: what was denied, the exact reason given, any codes or plan rules mentioned, and how and by when to appeal. Then list what I would need to show to answer that reason. If something is not stated, say so instead of guessing.

Copy the appeal deadline from the letter itself and put it on your calendar. Do not rely on the AI for dates.

Prompt 2

Build an evidence checklist

My claim for [service or treatment] was denied for this reason: [paste reason]. My plan documents say this about coverage for it: [paste the relevant section, or write 'not found yet']. Make a checklist of documents that would help an appeal, such as provider records, a doctor's support letter, prior authorization paperwork and the EOB. Mark which I have [list them] and which I still need, and from whom.

Ask your insurer for the claim file and the exact rule or guideline used to deny it.

Prompt 3

The appeal letter

Write a clear, polite appeal letter to [Insurance company] about a denied claim for [service] on [date of service], claim number [add it yourself later]. The denial reason was: [reason]. My response: [key points, e.g. it was pre-approved, the code was wrong]. Enclosed: [list]. Ask them to reverse the denial, or explain in writing if they uphold it. Under 400 words, factual, short headings, no threats.

Send it the way the denial letter says, keep a copy and note the date.

Prompt 4

Ask your doctor for a support letter

I need a letter of medical necessity from my [doctor or specialist] to support an insurance appeal. The insurer denied [service] saying: [reason]. Write a short, polite request to the doctor's office asking for a letter explaining why this service was needed and what other options were tried. Make it easy for a busy office to say yes.

Your doctor decides what goes in the letter. Give the office the denial and your deadline.

Prompt 5

Follow-up call and next steps

I sent an appeal to [Insurance company] on [date] and [have not heard back / they upheld the denial saying: paste reply]. Write a short phone script to check the status: the facts to state, what to ask (status, decision date, anything missing) and a line asking for the rep's name and a reference number. Then list options to ask about if the appeal is denied, such as external review. Do not invent deadlines.

If the appeal fails, ask about external review. Your state insurance department or a patient advocate can explain what applies.

Worked example

Situation: a member on [Plan name] is denied a scan their doctor ordered: not medically necessary, and no prior authorization on file.

What you paste: the denial letter, names and IDs removed, into Prompt 1. The summary shows two issues: no authorization on record, and a guideline about trying other treatment first.

Next: Prompt 2 lists the authorization reference from [Clinic name], notes on earlier treatment and a support letter. Prompt 4 requests the letter, and Prompt 3 drafts an appeal with one heading per reason. The member calendars the deadline from the denial letter. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of any result.

FAQ

Is it safe to paste an insurance denial letter into ChatGPT?

Remove your name, date of birth, member ID, claim numbers and health details the AI does not need. The denial reason, the service and the plan wording are usually enough. Check your AI tool's privacy settings too.

Can ChatGPT tell me if my appeal will win?

No. It cannot see your records or full plan, or know how your insurer applies its rules. It can help you answer the stated reason clearly. For coverage questions, your insurer or a patient advocate has the final word.

How long do I have to appeal a denied claim?

It depends on your plan and where you live. Check the denial letter and your plan documents for the exact deadline, and call member services if unclear. Start early: records and a doctor's letter take time.
