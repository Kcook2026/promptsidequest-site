ChatGPT prompt to dispute a tow bill

Five copy-paste prompts to sort out what happened, decode the charges on a tow or impound bill, list questions for the tow yard, draft a written dispute and keep a record.

Getting a car towed is stressful, and the bill often arrives as one total with a few short line items. Tow and storage rules, what fees are allowed and how long you have to challenge a charge depend on where you are and who ordered the tow, so the official source is your city, county or state rules, the tow company's posted information and the paperwork you were given. An AI chat cannot tell you what is allowed or promise any outcome.

What AI is good at is the paperwork: turning a pile of photos and receipts into a clear timeline, translating line items into plain English, listing questions to ask the yard and drafting a calm written dispute that quotes only what you can document. You confirm every rule and next step with the tow company, the agency that regulates towing where you are, or a local legal aid or consumer office.

Before pasting anything, remove your name, address, phone, license plate, VIN, driver license number and account or claim numbers. Dates, amounts and charge names as printed on the bill are enough. This page is not legal advice, and it does not state any fee limits, deadlines or legal rights.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Build a timeline from your own paperwork

I want to dispute a tow bill. Here is what I have, with my name, plate, VIN and contact details removed: [paste what the bill, receipt, signs, photos or messages say, with dates and times]. Turn this into a short, factual timeline: where the car was parked, who ordered or authorized the tow if I know, when I found out, when and where I picked it up, what I paid or was asked to pay, and what I have photos or receipts for. List separately what I do not know yet. Do not guess any facts or rules.

Take photos of the signage, the spot, the car and every receipt as early as you can. Keep the originals.

Prompt 2

Decode the charges line by line

Explain this tow or impound bill in plain English. Text with my name, plate and account numbers removed: [paste each line item with its amount and any notes]. Make a table: charge as written, plain-English meaning, whether it has a date or number of days attached, and a question to ask the yard about it. Flag any charge I cannot match to a service, any repeated charge and any line that is vague. Do not say whether a charge is legal or what a fair amount is. Only mark it ask the yard / check official rules.

Ask the yard for an itemized bill in writing if you only got a single total.

Prompt 3

Questions to ask the tow yard and the rules to check

Based on this timeline and bill: [paste from Prompts 1 and 2], write two lists. First, questions to ask the tow company, such as who authorized the tow, what posted signs or notices they relied on, how each charge was calculated, how storage is counted, what they accept as payment, how to get my belongings out of the car and how to dispute a charge. Second, a checklist of where to look for the official rules for my area, such as the city, county or state agency or office that handles towing or consumer complaints, and what to look for there. Do not state any fee limits, deadlines or legal rights as fact. Say look up and confirm instead.

Write down who you spoke to, the date and anything they say about how to dispute. Ask for it in writing.

Prompt 4

Write the dispute letter or message

Write a short, polite written dispute to [tow company] about the bill dated [date]. Use only these facts: [paste the timeline and the specific charges I question and why]. Say clearly which charges I am disputing, what I am asking for (for example an itemized explanation, a correction or a refund of specific charges), and ask them to reply in writing by [a date I choose] with the reason for each charge and how to appeal if I disagree. Mention that I have photos and receipts. Keep it under 200 words, calm and specific. Do not invent laws, fee limits, deadlines or threats, and do not claim anything I did not tell you.

Send it in a way that leaves a record, such as email or the form on the tow company's website, and keep a copy.

Prompt 5

Record log and follow-up

Help me keep a record of this tow bill dispute. Facts: date [ ], who I contacted [name and role], channel [phone / email / form / in person], what I asked, what they said, what I sent, any reference number [ ], what they said happens next, and a follow-up date. Build a simple saveable table with those columns and fill in this entry: [details]. Then draft a short polite follow-up for if I have not heard back by [date I choose], asking only for status and a written answer. Include a line reminding me to save photos, receipts and every message in one folder. No threats or legal claims.

If the yard does not resolve it, ask the agency you found in Prompt 3 or a local legal aid or consumer office what the next step is.

Worked example

Situation: someone finds their car gone from a lot, locates it at a tow yard and is handed a bill with a tow charge, a storage charge and an administrative fee. They are not sure the signage was clear.

What they do: they photograph the lot signs and the receipt, then paste the details (plate and name removed) into Prompt 1 for a timeline and Prompt 2 to decode each line. Prompt 3 gives questions for the yard and a checklist for looking up the local rules.

Next: they call or email the yard with those questions, send the Prompt 4 letter for the charges they still question, and use Prompt 5 to log every answer. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of any refund or other outcome.

FAQ

Can AI tell me if my tow was legal or if the fees are allowed?

No. Rules differ by place and by who ordered the tow. Check your city, county or state rules, the tow company's posted information and the agency that handles towing or consumer complaints where you are. These prompts only organize your facts, questions and drafts.

Is this legal advice?

No. These prompts help you prepare a timeline, questions, a letter and a record. For legal questions, contact a local legal aid office, a consumer protection office or a lawyer.

Is it safe to paste a tow bill into an AI chat?

Paste only what you need. Remove your name, address, phone, license plate, VIN, driver license number and any account or claim numbers first. Check your AI tool's privacy settings if you do not want chats saved.
