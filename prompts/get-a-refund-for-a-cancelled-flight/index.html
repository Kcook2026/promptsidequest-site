ChatGPT prompt to get an airline refund

Five copy-paste prompts to sort out what happened to your flight, find the rules that apply, ask for your money back in writing and follow up.

When an airline cancels your flight or changes it a lot, the app often pushes you toward a rebooking or a travel credit. Depending on the airline, the route and the country, you may be able to ask for your money back instead. The hard part is usually not the asking; it is knowing what to ask for, keeping the facts straight and following up without giving up.

The rules come from two places: the airline itself (its conditions of carriage and customer service plan, usually linked at the bottom of its website) and the government regulator for flights in that country or region, such as the U.S. Department of Transportation's aviation consumer pages or the passenger rights rules for flights in the UK and EU. Read those pages for your trip; they are the source, not an AI chat.

An AI chat cannot see your booking, check the airline's systems or tell you for certain what you are owed. It is good at turning messy emails and app notices into a clear timeline, listing the questions to check against the official rules and drafting a short, firm refund request. Remove your booking reference, card numbers, passport details and date of birth before you paste anything. This page is not legal advice.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Sort out what actually happened

Help me prepare an airline refund request. Here are the emails and app notices I got about my flight, with personal details removed: [paste them]. Turn this into a short, factual timeline: original flight and date, what the airline changed or cancelled and when they told me, what they offered (rebooking, credit, refund), and what I did. Then tell me in one sentence whether this looks like a cancellation, a significant schedule change, a delay or something I chose to change myself, and what I should double-check because you are unsure.

Screenshots of the app can disappear when a booking changes. Save the emails and take screenshots now.

Prompt 2

List what to check in the official rules

My flight was with [airline], from [city/country] to [city/country], booked through [the airline directly / a travel agency or booking site], and paid with [card / points / voucher]. Based on this timeline: [paste from Prompt 1], write a checklist of questions I should look up in the airline's own conditions of carriage and customer service plan and on the government regulator's website for this route. Include things like: does a cancellation or big schedule change entitle me to a refund if I do not travel, does accepting a credit or a new flight affect that, how long they say a refund takes, and who handles it if I booked through a third party. Do not state any amounts, time limits or rules as fact; just tell me what to look for and where.

Write down the page and the exact sentence you rely on, with the date you read it.

Prompt 3

Write the refund request

Write a short refund request to [airline / booking site] about booking [placeholder for reference]. Use this timeline: [paste from Prompt 1]. I want [a refund to my original payment method / an explanation of why I was offered only a credit]. Say clearly what happened, that I did not take the alternative they offered (if that is true), and what I am asking for. Quote this rule I found, word for word, with where it comes from: [paste the sentence and the page]. Ask for written confirmation and a date when the refund will be processed. Keep it under 200 words, polite and firm, with no threats and no claims about laws I have not quoted.

Send it through the airline's own refund form if it has one, and paste the letter into the comments box.

Prompt 4

Prepare for a phone or chat call

I am about to call or chat with [airline] about a refund. Here is my timeline: [paste] and what I am asking for: [paste]. Write a short script: how to open, the one sentence that says exactly what I want, how to answer if they offer a credit or a new flight instead, and how to ask for a reference number and a summary by email. Add three calm replies for when the agent says no, including asking for a supervisor or the written policy.

Note the agent's name, the time and the reference number before you hang up.

Prompt 5

Follow up and escalate

Make a simple tracking table for my airline refund with these columns: date, who I contacted, how (form, email, phone, chat), reference number, what they said, what I sent, and next step. Fill in this row: [details]. Then write a short follow-up message for when I have not heard back, and a separate message if they refuse, asking for the reason in writing and the next step in their complaints process. Finally, list where I could take it next if the airline does not resolve it, such as the regulator's complaint form for this route, the card issuer for a payment dispute, or the booking site, and what I should check before doing each one.

Check the card issuer's own dispute rules and time limits before you file anything with them.

Worked example

Situation: a traveler's morning flight is cancelled the night before, and the app automatically rebooks them on a flight the next afternoon, which no longer works for their trip.

What you paste: the cancellation email and the rebooking notice into Prompt 1. The AI builds a timeline showing the airline cancelled, offered a next-day flight and that the traveler has not used it.

Next: Prompt 2 gives them a checklist to read on the airline's customer service plan and the regulator's refund page; they copy the sentence that covers their case. Prompt 3 drafts a short request through the airline's refund form quoting that sentence, and Prompt 5 sets a follow-up date. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of any outcome.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT get my airline refund for me?

No. It can help you organize what happened, find what to check and draft the request, but you submit it yourself through the airline or booking site, and the airline decides. Always check the airline's own policy and the regulator's rules for your route.

Should I accept a travel credit or a new flight?

Read the airline's offer and the official rules first. In some cases accepting an alternative may change what you can ask for later, so it is worth checking before you click accept.

What if I booked through a travel site?

Ask both the booking site and the airline who handles the refund, and get the answer in writing. Use Prompt 5 to keep track of who said what and when.
