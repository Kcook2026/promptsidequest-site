ChatGPT prompt to audit my subscriptions

Five copy-paste prompts to pull recurring charges from your statements, flag overlaps and unused services from what you say you use, decide keep / pause / cancel, and draft a polite cancel or downgrade message.

Streaming apps, cloud storage, fitness apps and forgotten free trials can stack up quietly. An audit is a clear list plus a decision for each item: keep, pause, downgrade or cancel.

An AI chat is useful for the sorting work. Paste merchant names, dates and amounts (with account and card numbers removed), say which services you actually use, and it can group them, flag likely overlaps, and help you draft cancellation or downgrade messages. It cannot log into your accounts, know a company's refund policy, or promise how much you will save. Those answers come from your own statements and each service's cancellation page.

Before pasting anything, remove account numbers, card numbers, full names and addresses. Merchant names, dates and amounts are enough. Check each service's own cancellation and billing rules before you cancel. This page is not financial or legal advice.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Extract every recurring charge

Here are transactions from my bank and card statements for [time period, e.g. the last 90 days], with account numbers, card numbers and my name removed: [paste merchant names, dates and amounts]. List every charge that looks like a subscription, membership, streaming, app, software, cloud storage, fitness, news or free trial converting to paid. Make a table with: merchant, amount, how often (monthly, yearly, other), most recent charge date, and a blank keep / pause / cancel column. Add monthly and yearly totals using only amounts I pasted. Mark unclear lines instead of guessing.

Three months of statements usually catches quarterly and yearly renewals. Export or copy lines from your bank and card apps; do not paste full account numbers.

Prompt 2

Group by category and flag overlaps

Using only this subscription list: [paste the table from Prompt 1 or your own list]. And using only what I tell you I actually use: [list services you use regularly, occasionally, or not at all]. Group them into categories (streaming, music, software, cloud, fitness, news, shopping memberships, other). Flag likely overlaps or items I said I do not use. Do not invent prices or savings. For each flagged item, ask me one short question before recommending cancel.

Your answers matter more than the AI's guesses. If you still watch one show on a service, say so.

Prompt 3

Keep / pause / cancel decision table

Help me decide what to do with these subscriptions. List: [paste services with amount and how often]. My priorities: [e.g. cut unused ones first, keep family shared plans, keep one streaming service, pause instead of cancel when I might return]. Ask any missing questions briefly, then build a table: service, amount, decision (keep / pause / downgrade / cancel), why in one sentence, next step. Use only my facts. Do not invent pause or downgrade options; mark those as 'check the service's account page'.

A decision table beats a vague "cut subscriptions" goal. Fill the next-step column before you start cancelling.

Prompt 4

Draft a cancel or downgrade message

I want to [cancel / downgrade / pause] my [service name] [plan name if known] on the account under [email you signed up with, add it yourself]. Here is what I know about their process: [paste steps from their help page, or write 'I have not checked yet']. Write a short, polite message or chat script that states what I want, asks for written confirmation of the effective date and last charge, and skips retention offers unless I say otherwise. Under 120 words. Do not invent refund rights, notice periods or fees.

Use the company's own cancel flow first when you can. Save a screenshot or email of the confirmation.

Prompt 5

Monthly subscription budget checklist

Help me keep subscriptions under control after payday. I get paid on [pay dates]. My keep list is: [services I am keeping, with amounts if known]. My cancel or pause list is: [services I decided to cut]. Make a short checklist: which cancellations to finish first, what confirmation to save, which renewal reminders to set, and how to add the keep-list total as one budget line. Do not invent due dates; use [dates I know] or mark unknowns.

Pair this with a monthly budget so the keep-list total is a planned line, not a surprise.

Worked example

Situation: someone notices several streaming charges plus a gym membership they have not used and a free trial that converted to a paid plan after they forgot the end date.

What they do: Prompt 1 turns 90 days of statements into a table of recurring charges. Prompt 2 groups two overlapping streaming services and flags the unused gym and the converted trial based only on what they said they use. Prompt 3 builds a keep / pause / cancel table.

Next: Prompt 4 drafts a cancel message for the unused service using steps they copied from that company's help page, and Prompt 5 turns the keep list into a payday checklist and a single budget line. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of savings or refunds.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT cancel my subscriptions for me?

No. A regular AI chat cannot log into your accounts or click cancel. It helps you list charges, decide what to cut and draft messages. You still cancel or downgrade through each service's website, app, app store or support channel, and you should follow that service's own steps.

Will I save a specific amount if I audit my subscriptions?

Nobody can promise a savings figure without your statements and decisions. Add up amounts you choose to cancel or pause using your own numbers. Some companies offer a pause or cheaper plan; check each service's account page rather than assuming.

Is it safe to paste my bank statement into an AI chat?

Paste only merchant names, dates and amounts. Remove account numbers, card numbers, full names and addresses first. Check your AI tool's privacy settings if you do not want chats saved or used for training.
