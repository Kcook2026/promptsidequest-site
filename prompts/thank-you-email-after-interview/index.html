ChatGPT prompt to write a thank-you email after an interview

Five copy-paste prompts that turn your interview notes into a short, specific thank-you email, a polite follow-up if the line goes quiet, and a gracious reply whatever the answer is.

The interview is over, you are tired, and now you are supposed to write something that sounds warm but not desperate, specific but not long. Most people either skip it or send the same three generic lines every hiring manager has seen a hundred times.

An AI chat is good at this job if you give it the right raw material. The trick is to jot down a few notes right after the interview: who you spoke with, one or two things they said that stuck with you, and anything you wish you had said better. The AI can turn those notes into a short email that sounds like you and proves you were listening.

Keep it honest. Do not let the AI invent things you did not discuss or skills you do not have, and read every draft out loud before you send it. A two-minute edit to make it sound like your own voice matters more than a perfect sentence.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

The thank-you email

Write a thank-you email after a job interview. Role: [job title] at [company]. I spoke with [name and title of each interviewer]. Interview type: [phone / video / in person], on [day]. Things they said that stood out: [one or two specific points, e.g. the team is moving to a new system, they need someone who can train new staff]. Why I am a good fit, in my words: [one or two sentences]. Keep it under 150 words, warm and professional, no flattery, no clichés like 'I am writing to express'. Give me a subject line and end with a simple sign-off.

The core prompt. One real detail from the conversation does more than any amount of polish.

Prompt 2

One email per interviewer

I interviewed with [number] people at [company] for [role]: [name, title and one thing each person focused on]. Write a separate short thank-you email for each one, under 120 words each. Each email should mention something specific to that person's part of the interview, so they do not read like copies if they compare notes. Keep my tone [friendly / formal].

Panels often compare notes, so identical emails stand out for the wrong reason.

Prompt 3

Fix the answer you fumbled

In my interview for [role] I was asked [question], and my answer was weak because [what went wrong, e.g. I blanked on an example]. Here is what I wish I had said: [your better answer or rough notes]. Add one or two calm sentences to my thank-you email that briefly add this point without sounding like I am apologizing or over-explaining. Here is my draft: [paste draft].

Use it for one point at most. A thank-you email is not a second interview.

Prompt 4

The polite follow-up

I interviewed for [role] at [company] on [date] and sent a thank-you email on [date]. They said I would hear back by [date or 'no date given'], and it has now been [time]. Write a short, polite follow-up email under 90 words that checks on the timeline, restates my interest in one sentence, and makes it easy for them to reply. No guilt-tripping and no pressure.

Waiting about a week past the date they gave you is a common, reasonable rule of thumb.

Prompt 5

Reply to the answer, either way

I just heard back about the [role] at [company]. The answer was [offer / rejection / they want another interview]. Write a short, gracious reply. If it is an offer, thank them and ask for the details in writing and a reasonable time to decide. If it is a rejection, thank them, keep the door open and ask if they would share any brief feedback. If it is another interview, confirm my availability: [times].

A kind reply to a rejection is remembered more often than you would think.

Worked example

Situation: an example candidate has a video interview for an office manager role with two people, and the hiring manager mentions twice that the team is struggling with a messy shared calendar.

What you paste: the role, both names and titles, the calendar problem, and a one-line note that they set up a shared booking system at a previous job, into Prompt 1. The AI writes a 120-word email that thanks the manager, refers to the calendar problem and mentions the booking system in one sentence.

Next: Prompt 2 writes a shorter, different note for the second interviewer, who focused on budgets. The candidate reads both out loud, changes two phrases that do not sound like them, and sends them the same afternoon.

FAQ

Should I send a thank-you email after an interview?

It is a widely recommended habit. A short, specific thank-you email is quick to write, shows you were listening, and gives you one more chance to connect your experience to what the team needs. Keep it brief and honest rather than long and formal.

How soon should I send it?

Many people aim for the same day or within 24 hours, while the conversation is still fresh for both sides. Write your notes right after the interview so the AI has real details to work with.

Can I use ChatGPT to write my thank-you email?

Yes, as long as the details are true and the final version sounds like you. Give the AI your real notes, ask for a short draft, then edit it in your own words. Never let it add skills, experience or conversation points that did not happen.
