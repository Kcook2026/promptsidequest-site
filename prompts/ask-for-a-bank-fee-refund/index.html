ChatGPT prompt to get an overdraft fee refunded

Five copy-paste prompts to spot the fee on your statement, check what your own account terms say, ask your bank politely by phone or message, and stop the same fee from happening again.

An overdraft or bank fee can feel like a penalty on top of a tight month. Many people never ask about it, but banks and credit unions often have their own process for reviewing a fee, especially when it is a first time, a timing mix-up or a short-lived dip below zero. What your bank will do depends on its own policy and your account history, so nobody can promise a refund. Asking clearly and politely is free.

An AI chat is good at the preparation: finding exactly which fees you were charged, turning dense account terms into plain English, writing a short call script or secure message, and building a simple plan so it does not happen again. It cannot tell you what your bank is required to do. That comes from your account agreement, your bank's fee schedule and, if you think something is wrong, your bank's complaint process.

Before pasting anything, remove your account number, card number, full name and address. The fee lines, dates and amounts are enough. This page is not legal or financial advice.

The prompts

Replace every [bracket] with your real details, then paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok.

Prompt 1

Find the fees on your statement

Here are the transactions from my bank statement for [month], with account numbers and my name removed: [paste the lines]. List every fee you can see (overdraft, insufficient funds, returned item, monthly maintenance, transfer, ATM or other), with the date, amount and the transaction that seems to have triggered it. Then add up the total fees for the month. If a line is unclear, say so instead of guessing what it is.

If your statement only shows a short code, your bank's app or a customer service rep can tell you what that fee is called.

Prompt 2

Read your account terms in plain English

Here is the fee section of my bank account agreement or fee schedule: [paste the text]. Using only this text, explain in plain English: what triggers an overdraft or insufficient funds fee, whether there is a daily limit on how many can be charged, any grace period or buffer, any overdraft protection option, and any mention of fee reviews, waivers or refunds. Quote the sentence each point comes from, and say clearly when the text does not answer a question.

You can usually find the fee schedule on your bank's website, in the app or by asking a branch for a copy of the current one.

Prompt 3

Call script to ask for a refund

I am going to call [bank or credit union name] to ask them to refund [fee type] of [amount] charged on [date]. Context: [why it happened, e.g. a paycheck landed a day late, a timing mix-up, a one-off mistake], how long I have banked with them: [years], and whether this has happened before: [yes/no]. Write me a short, polite script that explains what happened in two sentences, asks plainly whether they can refund or waive the fee, and gives calm replies if they say no, offer a partial refund or say they need to review it. End by asking for a reference number and the name of the person I spoke to.

Be friendly and brief. The person on the phone often has some say, and a calm, clear request is easier to say yes to.

Prompt 4

Secure message or letter version

Write a short, polite message I can send through my bank's secure messaging or app chat asking them to review and refund [fee type] of [amount] charged on [date]. Include: what happened in plain words: [details], that I have now [brought the account back above zero / moved money in], how long I have been a customer: [years], and a request for a written reply. Keep it under 120 words and do not claim the bank did anything wrong unless I tell you it did.

Written messages give you a record. Save a screenshot of what you sent and any reply in the same folder as your statement.

Prompt 5

Plan so it does not happen again

Help me avoid another overdraft fee. My account gets paid on [pay dates], and these bills come out automatically: [list bills with usual dates and rough amounts]. My lowest balance usually hits around [date]. Suggest a simple plan: which due dates to ask to move, which low-balance alerts to switch on in my banking app, whether to ask my bank about overdraft protection options or opting out of overdraft coverage, and a small buffer target. Turn it into a short checklist I can do this week. Do not assume what options my bank offers; phrase those as questions to ask them.

Low-balance alerts and moving a bill's due date a few days after payday are often the quickest wins.

Worked example

Situation: someone's paycheck landed a day later than usual because of a holiday, an automatic phone bill payment came out first, and the account dipped below zero overnight, triggering an overdraft fee.

What they do: Prompt 1 lists the fee and the payment that triggered it. Prompt 2 shows the account terms mention fee reviews but do not promise a refund. Prompt 3 gives them a two-sentence explanation and a polite request to use on a call.

Next: whatever the bank decides, they note the reference number, then use Prompt 5 to switch on a low-balance alert and ask the phone company to move the bill a few days after payday. This is an illustrative example, not a promise of any outcome.

FAQ

Will my bank refund an overdraft fee if I ask?

Some banks and credit unions will review a fee and refund it, especially for a first time or a timing mix-up, but it depends on the bank's own policy and your account history. Nobody can promise an outcome, so ask politely, explain briefly what happened and ask for a reference number either way.

Should I call or send a message?

Either can work. A call lets you explain quickly and hear the answer straight away, while a secure message or letter gives you a written record. Many people call first and then save notes, or send a message if they prefer writing.

How do I stop overdraft fees from happening again?

Look at when your pay arrives compared with when automatic bills come out, switch on low-balance alerts in your banking app, and ask your bank what overdraft options it offers, including protection or opting out of coverage. Prompt 5 turns that into a checklist.
